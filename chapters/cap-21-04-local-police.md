Act 13, 1972, Act 24, 1979, Act 25, 1984. An Act to provide for the establishment of a Local Police Force; to provide for the appointment and discipline of officers of such force; to set out the powers of such officers and to provide for all matters incidental to the establishment, control and administration of such Force. [Date of Commencement: 19th May, 1972]

### Section 1 — Short title

This Act may be cited as the Local Police Act.

### Section 2 — Interpretation

In this Act, except where the context otherwise requires— "Force" means the Local Police Force established under section 3; "officer" means an officer of the Force; "sub-chief" means a sub-chief recognized as such under section 19 of the Chieftainship Act.

### Section 3 — Establishment of the Force

(1) There is hereby established a Force, to be known as the Local Police Force.

(2) The President may, by order published in the Gazette, appoint such number of officers of the Force as may be considered necessary for the purposes of this Act.

(3) The Minister shall be in charge of the Force and shall, by notice published in the Gazette, assign such number of officers appointed under subsection (2) to any tribal area or tribalcommunity area as may be considered necessary.

(4) The Force shall consist of officers of the ranks set forth in the First Schedule.

(5) An officer of the Force shall be a peace officer for the purposes of the written laws of Botswana.

### Section 4 — Supervision of officers

(1) In every tribal area and tribal community area there shall be a committee consisting of the District Commissioner,who shall be chairman, a member, appointed by the Minister, of the local district council and the Chief or sub-chief of such area,as the case may be, which shall, subject to such directions as may be given by the Minister, be responsible for the general supervision of officers in its area.

(2) The President may, in appointing officers under section 3 (2) for any tribal area or tribal community area, as the case may be, consult the committee in such area.

(3) Every officer appointed by the President shall be issued with a certificate of appointment in the form set out in the Second Schedule and such certificate shall, subject to the provisions of subsection (5), be signed by the District Commissioner within whose jurisdiction such area is.

(4) A certificate of appointment issued to any officer shall,until the contrary is proved, be prima facie evidence of his appointment under this Act.

(5) No certificate shall be issued unless the officer has made and signed a declaration before the District Commissioner in the form set out in the Third Schedule.

### Section 5 — Employment of local police

The Force shall be employed in Botswana for the performance of the duties set out in this Act.

### Section 6 — Administration of local police in districts

The Chief or sub-chief of any area within which officers have been appointed shall administer the Force in his area subject to the general or special directions of the Minister.

### Section 7 — Local police ordinarily empowered to act in own area

An officer shall carry out the duties imposed upon him by this Act within the area to which he is appointed: Provided that—

(i) with the consent of the Chief or sub-chief of another area, he shall execute such duties in such other area;

(ii) he may, without any consent, enter any adjoining area for the purpose of exercising the powers of arrest exercisable by him by virtue of being a peace officer or of protecting life or property.

### Section 8 — Duties of officers

Every officer shall—

(a) when called upon by any Chief or sub-chief, assist him in the exercise of his lawful duties;

(b) obey and execute promptly all orders and warrants lawfully issued to him by any competent authority;

(c) preserve the public peace, prevent the commission of offences and apprehend all persons in respect of whom he holds a valid warrant of arrest;

(d) undergo such training as may be ordered by the Minister;

(e) act as messenger in any matter connected with tribal administration, customary courts or the public service;

(f) assist any district council in carrying out its lawful functions when called upon to do so:

Provided that the Minister may direct that a district council and a Chief or subchief shall not have power to call upon officers to assist it or him or may only call upon officers to assist it or him for specified purposes.

### Section 9 — Co-operation, etc., with Botswana Police Force

The Force shall at all times co-operate with the Botswana Police Force and shall, if the President so directs in writing, be subject to the orders of the Commissioner of Police.

### Section 10 — Power to lay information or complaint

An officer may lay any information or complaint before any customary court and may make application to such court for a summons, warrant or other legal process.

### Section 11 — Power to stop vehicles, etc., without warrant

(1) An officer may stop, search and detain any vehicle or vessel which he has reasonable cause to suspect is being employed in the commission of, or to facilitate the commission of, an offence.

(2) Any person who fails to obey a reasonable signal given by an officer in uniform requiring him to stop any vehicle or vessel shall be guilty of an offence and liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months, and an officer may arrest such person without a warrant unless he gives his name and address and otherwise satisfies such officer that he will duly answer any summons or other proceedings which may issue or be taken against him.

(3) No person shall be entitled to damages or compensation for any loss or damage suffered by him by reason of any action taken in pursuance of this section.

### Section 12

Power to stop and detain any person for production of licence, etc.

(1) An officer in uniform may stop and detain any person whom he—

(a) sees doing any act or thing;

(b) sees in possession of any thing; or

(c) suspects of doing any act or thing or of being in possession of any thing, for which a licence, permit or certificate is required under any written law and may require such person to produce such licence, permit or certificate.

(2) Any person who fails to produce such licence, permit or certificate when called upon by an officer to do so may be arrested without a warrant unless he gives his name and address and otherwise satisfies such officer that he will duly answer any summons or other proceedings which may issue or be taken against him.

### Section 13 — Power to erect barriers

(1) A Chief or sub-chief may, if he considers it necessary to do so for the maintenance and preservation of law and order or for the prevention or detection of any offence, order an officer to erect or place barriers in or across any road or street or in any public place.

(2) Any officer in uniform may take all reasonable steps to prevent any vehicle from being driven past any such barrier and any driver who fails to comply with any reasonable signal given by an officer in uniform requiring him to stop such vehicle before any such barrier shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine not exceeding P100 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 12 months, or to both.

(3) No officer shall be liable for any loss or damage resulting to any vehicle or for any injury to any occupant of any such vehicle as the result of the driver of such vehicle failing to obey any officer acting under the provisions of subsection (2).

### Section 14 — Custody of unclaimed property

Every officer shall take charge of all unclaimed property handed to him by any person or found by him to be unclaimed and shall deliver such property without delay to the nearest Botswana Police Force station.

### Section 15 — Non-liability for act done under authority of a warrant

(1) Where the defence to any suit against an officer is that the act complained of was done in obedience to a warrant purporting to be issued by a customary court, the court shall, upon production of the warrant, accept such warrant as prima facie evidence of the due making thereof and, upon proof that the act complained of was done in obedience to such warrant, enter judgment in favour of such officer.

(2) No proof of the signature upon such warrant shall be required unless the court has reason to doubt its genuineness,and, where such signature is proved not to be genuine, the court shall nevertheless enter judgment in favour of the officer if it is satisfied that, at the time of the act complained of; such officer believed, on reasonable grounds, that the signature was genuine.

### Section 16

Salary not liable to be attached No salary or other allowance paid to any officer shall be liable to be attached, sequestrated or levied upon, for or in respect of; any debt or claim for money borrowed by him or any goods supplied to him or to any person on his behalf while such officer is a member of the Force.

### Section 17 — Assault in execution of duty

Any person who assaults, resists or wilfully obstructs any officer in the due execution of his duty or any person acting in aid of such officer shall be guilty of an offence and liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years.

### Section 18 — Penalties for offences by officers

(1) Any officer who wilfully disobeys any lawful order or warrant or who is guilty of any act or conduct to the prejudice of good order or discipline shall be liable to be punished by a committee established under section 4 by a—

(a) reprimand;

(b) fine not exceeding the equivalent of seven days' pay;

(c) reduction in rank;

(d) stoppage of salary increments;

(e) dismissal from the Force, or by any combination of such punishments: Provided that any sentence of dismissal from the Force shall be subject to confirmation by the Minister.

(2) A committee awarding any punishment inflicted upon an officer under this section shall enter a record of such punishment, the date thereof and the offence for which it was inflicted on the record sheet of such local police officer.

(3) All fines imposed by a committee under the provisions of this section shall be recoverable by monthly deductions from the pay due and thereafter becoming due to the officer.

(4) The amount of such deduction in respect of any fine shall, subject to any general or special directions by the Minister, lie in the discretion of the committee imposing the fine, but whenever more than one order for deduction from an officer's salary is inforce, so much only of his salary may be deducted as shall leave such officer not less than two-thirds of his salary.

### Section 19 — Rules

The Minister may make rules for the better carrying into effect of the provisions of this Act.

### Section 20 — Standing Orders

The Minister may issue Standing Orders for the general control, direction and administration of the Force.
